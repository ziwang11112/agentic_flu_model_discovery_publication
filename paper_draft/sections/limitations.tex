\section{Discussion}

The main finding is not that a particular model family dominates. The
framework produces a more useful output: a map of where recommendations are
stable, where they are objective-dependent, and where claims must be narrowed.
In the synthetic tasks, the policy layer can recover known observation and
delay labels under controlled conditions. In candidate-budget experiments,
structured orderings reach useful candidates faster than fixed
no-observation baselines and more reliably than random orderings, although
random remains competitive in some settings. In the provider benchmark, four
LLM provider families can be routed through the same constrained proposer
protocol, but single-shot is often as strong as iterative refinement in a
small allowlisted space. In FluSurv-NET, pediatric \texttt{0-4 yr} has the
clearest observation-aware discovery evidence, while adult strata often favor
simpler baselines.

This is precisely the purpose of verifier-gated selection. Agreement across
forecasting baselines, mechanistic baselines, structured search, ablations,
proposers, and policies supports a stronger recommendation. Disagreement is
not a failure of the framework; it is evidence that the claim boundary should
be narrowed.

\section{Limitations}

The paper is not a FluSight leaderboard result and does not evaluate
operational forecasting systems. The FluSurv-NET benchmark is retrospective,
bounded by the chosen grammar, and uses a frozen artifact package. The
multi-season appendix is a reduced-budget robustness check, not
previous-season-to-future-season transfer forecasting. Synthetic tasks are
generic software validation tasks and do not prove real-world mechanism
recovery. Bounded real-data execution uses reduced fitting/search budgets and
supports budget-efficiency evidence only. The cross-provider benchmark is a
structured-proposer benchmark, not a provider ranking for forecasting or
clinical use. Finally, the framework is not a fully autonomous scientist:
language models propose allowlisted candidate records, while deterministic
code performs verification, evaluation, selection, and claim auditing.

\section{Conclusion}

We introduced a verifier-gated, evidence-aware framework for model selection
under partial observation. The framework integrates constrained structured
proposal, hard verification, no-leakage evidence use, multi-policy selection,
synthetic recovery, candidate-budget evaluation, cross-provider proposer
benchmarking, and claim-boundary auditing. Across the experiments, the result
is a cautious but useful recommendation story: observation-aware structured
search is most compelling for pediatric \texttt{0-4 yr}; adult strata often
favor simpler baselines or manual mechanistic models; and cross-season
evidence is mixed. More broadly, the framework shows how heterogeneous
forecasting evidence can be turned into auditable recommendations without
claiming a universal winner.
