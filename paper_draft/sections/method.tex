\section{Framework}

\subsection{Problem setup}

Let $y_{1:T}$ denote a univariate time series split chronologically into
training, validation, and held-out test windows
$\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{tr}}$, $\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{val}}$, and
$\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{test}}$. Candidate proposal and model selection may use
only training, validation, and non-test rolling-validation evidence. Held-out
test evidence is computed only after selection.

For structured candidates, the observed series is modeled as a scaled noisy
view of a latent state:
\begin{equation}
y_t = \rho\,g_o(x_{t-d}) + \epsilon_t,
\label{eq:obs}
\end{equation}
where $x_t$ is the latent state, $g_o$ is an allowed observation map, $d$ is an
allowed delay, and $\rho$ is a scale parameter. In the bounded grammar used in
the case study, $g_o$ may select $I_t$, $H_t$, $I_t+H_t$, or a delayed
infectious component. Equation~\ref{eq:obs} is a modeling interface, not a
claim that the true data-generating mechanism has been recovered.

\subsection{Candidate space and proposer protocol}

The candidate space is cross-family:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C} =
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{forecast}} \cup
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{mech}} \cup
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{search}} \cup
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{ablation}}.
\end{equation}
The families contain forecasting baselines, hand-specified mechanistic
baselines, bounded structured-search candidates, and same-grammar ablations.
A candidate record is
\begin{equation}
c = (f, m, o, d, r),
\end{equation}
where $f$ is the model family, $m$ is the model name, $o$ is the observation
label, $d$ is the delay label, and $r$ is a short rationale or intended role.

At round $r$, a proposer $\pi_\theta$ receives a no-leakage context $q_r$ and
remaining budget $B_r$ and emits at most $b$ candidates:
\begin{equation}
\pi_\theta(q_r,B_r)\rightarrow \{c_{r,1},\ldots,c_{r,b}\}.
\end{equation}
The constrained agentic protocol has four tasks: initial candidate proposal,
evidence-aware refinement, failure diagnosis or ablation request, and
claim-boundary summary. Allowed context includes series metadata, the
candidate allowlist, budget, previous verifier decisions, accepted and
rejected candidates, duplicate candidates, family and observation-label
coverage, non-test rolling or validation evidence, numerical-risk warnings,
and remaining budget. Forbidden context includes held-out test MAE or RMSE,
test winners, test ranks, post-selection test comparisons, and paper
recommendations derived from test evidence.

\subsection{Verifier, evidence, and selection}

The verifier is a conjunction of hard checks:
\begin{equation}
V(c,q_r) =
V_{\mathrm{schema}}\wedge
V_{\mathrm{allowlist}}\wedge
V_{\mathrm{obs}}\wedge
V_{\mathrm{budget}}\wedge
V_{\mathrm{noLeak}}\wedge
V_{\mathrm{claim}}.
\end{equation}
It rejects malformed records, duplicate identifiers, out-of-allowlist models,
incompatible observation or delay labels, absolute artifact paths, executable
code fields, selection evidence that contains held-out test metrics, and
numerically flagged rows used as positive evidence.

For verified candidate $c$, the selection evidence is
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{sel}}(c)=
(\mathrm{val\_mae},\mathrm{rolling\_mae},
\mathrm{complexity},\mathrm{numerical\_risk},\mathrm{family}).
\end{equation}
When validation MAE is unavailable in compact replay rows, policies use rolling
evidence and mark that limitation. Held-out test evidence
$E_{\mathrm{test}}(c^\star)$ is attached only after a candidate $c^\star$ has
been selected and is labeled post-hoc descriptive.

The main policy, Pareto-epsilon, forms an epsilon Pareto frontier over
minimized objectives. For a budgeted ordering $c_1,\ldots,c_k$, define
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{hit}_{\epsilon}(k)
= \mathbb{1}\{\exists i\leq k: c_i\in \mathcal{T}_{\epsilon}\},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{T}_{\epsilon}$ is the top-epsilon reference set, and
\begin{equation}
B_{\epsilon} = \min\{k:\operatorname{hit}_{\epsilon}(k)=1\}.
\end{equation}
We also report a deterministic weighted-score ablation,
\begin{equation}
S_w(c)=\sum_j w_j\tilde{z}_j(c),
\end{equation}
over normalized lower-is-better objectives $\tilde{z}_j$, and a hard-veto
decision tree that prioritizes invalid-candidate removal, simple-baseline
sufficiency, observation-search ablation evidence, and numerical safety.

Finally, a claim auditor maps evidence into allowed claims, rejected claims,
and caveats:
\begin{equation}
A(E)\rightarrow
(\mathrm{allowed\_claims},\mathrm{rejected\_claims},\mathrm{caveats}).
\end{equation}
This auditor is intentionally conservative: it can allow group-specific or
budget-efficiency statements while rejecting global winner, state-of-the-art,
autonomous-science, provider-superiority, and real-world mechanism-recovery
claims.

\subsection{Model families and discovery scores}

Persistence predicts $\hat{y}_{T+h}=y_T$. A rolling mean with window $w$ uses
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_{T+h}=\frac{1}{w}\sum_{j=0}^{w-1}y_{T-j}.
\end{equation}
The small ARIMA baseline selects among a fixed order set by validation MAE.
Mechanistic baselines include deterministic SEIR, probabilistic SEIR,
hospitalized SEIHR, delayed-observation SEIR, and fractional SEIR variants.

The bounded structured-search grammar includes
$\{\mathrm{SIR},\mathrm{SEIR},\mathrm{SEIRS},\mathrm{SEIHR},\mathrm{SEIAR}\}$,
fractional on/off variants, observation maps $\{I,H,I+H,\mathrm{delayed}\_I\}$,
and bounded delays. The main discovery score is
\begin{align}
S_{\mathrm{stable}} &=
\overline{\mathrm{MAE}}_{\mathrm{multi\ split}}
+P_{\mathrm{multi\ split}}+P_{\mathrm{stability}}
+P_{\mathrm{complexity}}+P_{\mathrm{age}},\\
S_{\mathrm{validation}} &= \mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{val}},\\
S_{\mathrm{no\ stability}} &=
\overline{\mathrm{MAE}}_{\mathrm{multi\ split}}
+P_{\mathrm{multi\ split}}+P_{\mathrm{complexity}}+P_{\mathrm{age}}.
\end{align}
The no-observation-search ablation restricts the search to the direct
infectious observation map.
