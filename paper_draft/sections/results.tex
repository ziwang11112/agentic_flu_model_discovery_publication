\section{Results}

\subsection{RQ1: verification and claim-boundary auditing}

The verifier rejects the full negative set of unsafe candidate records, giving
a negative-set rejection rate of \num{1.0000}. Rejected records include
missing required fields, duplicate candidate identifiers, absolute artifact
paths, invalid observation or delay labels, held-out test metrics used as
selection evidence, and numerical-failure rows used as positive evidence.
Prompt and feedback audits also pass in the downstream experiments: the
bounded real-data execution audit contains 52 rows and all pass, and the
provider benchmark records no test-evidence prompt failures in the committed
compact outputs.

The claim auditor labels the frozen evidence as supporting no single global
winner, group-specific signals, simple-baseline competitiveness, and
post-hoc comparisons not being selection evidence. It rejects global-winner,
state-of-the-art, provider-superiority, autonomous-science,
flagged-row-positive, and real-world mechanism-recovery claims. This result is
not ancillary bookkeeping: it defines the admissible scope of the subsequent
recommendations.

\subsection{RQ2: synthetic structured recovery}

In the expanded synthetic sweep, Pareto-epsilon recovers the observation label
at rate \num{0.7429}, the delay label at rate \num{0.7470}, and reaches
top-epsilon hit rate \num{0.8504}. The random-label baseline has
observation-label recovery \num{0.2597}, and the no-observation-label baseline
has recovery \num{0.2000}. At noise level \num{0.10}, Pareto observation
recovery remains \num{0.6800}. Its budget curve rises from \num{0.5720} at
budget 3 to \num{0.8356} at budget 10 and remains stable for larger budgets.

The result is a recovery validation, not a real-world mechanism claim.
Pareto-epsilon is also not universally best: the deterministic seed proposer
has observation-label recovery \num{0.8257}. The strongest controlled evidence
appears on lagged-signal tasks, where policies that can select lagged
observations outperform the observation-fixed baseline. Mixture and proxy
tasks remain harder and expose the limits of the current candidate labels.

\subsection{RQ3: candidate-budget execution and bounded real-data execution}

Candidate-budget evaluation asks whether proposer orderings expose useful
candidates quickly. In synthetic candidate execution, the mock agentic proposer
has observation recovery \num{0.9313}, delay recovery \num{0.9190}, and
top-epsilon hit rate \num{1.0000}. The failure-guided proposer is similar
(\num{0.9233}, \num{0.9113}, \num{1.0000}). Random ordering is competitive but
less stable (\num{0.8208}, \num{0.7943}, \num{0.9045}); deterministic seed
reaches \num{0.7863}, \num{0.7278}, and \num{0.8453}; the no-observation
baseline remains weak (\num{0.2000}, \num{0.4000}, \num{0.3210}).

In frozen real-data replay, mock agent and oracle orderings both reach
top-epsilon rate \num{1.0000}, with mean rolling scores \num{0.0851} and
\num{0.0830}. Random reaches \num{0.8125} and \num{0.0925}; deterministic seed
reaches \num{0.7500} and \num{0.0949}. In bounded real-data execution,
deterministic seed, failure-guided, mock agent, and oracle reference reach
top-epsilon hit rate \num{1.0000}; random reaches \num{0.7500}. Bounded
execution required 32 unique real-data model executions and wrote only compact
summaries. These results support proposal-ordering and budget-efficiency
claims, not new forecasting-performance claims.

The provider result is best read as a protocol-stability result. The same
verifier-gated task can route multiple provider families through a shared
schema and no-leakage guard. Single-shot proposal often matches or exceeds
iterative proposal in this small explicit allowlist; iterative feedback is not
automatically superior. The benchmark therefore supports proposer-quality and
replay-efficiency claims only.

\subsection{RQ5: FluSurv-NET case study and discovery ablations}

The FluSurv-NET case study does not support a single global winner. The
pediatric \texttt{0-4 yr} series provides the strongest observation-aware
discovery signal: constrained structure discovery is selected as
stability-preferred, with an SEIRS structure, delayed-infectious observation
map, and one-week delay. The no-observation-search ablation gives direct
evidence that the observation map matters in this stratum. In paired
rolling-origin comparisons, constrained discovery has lower rolling error than
the no-observation-search ablation for \texttt{0-4 yr}, with mean difference
\num{0.0564} and 95\% interval $[\num{0.0173},\num{0.1082}]$ for challenger
minus reference.

Adult strata often favor simpler forecasting baselines or hand-specified
mechanistic baselines. The \texttt{18-49 yr} series is a consensus ARIMA case,
and ARIMA has lower rolling error than constrained discovery with mean
difference \num{-0.0487} and 95\% interval
$[\num{-0.0641},\num{-0.0323}]$. Other strata show objective dependence:
\texttt{>=65 yr} has a different held-out test winner and rolling-origin
winner, and \texttt{50-64 yr} similarly depends on the chosen criterion.

The discovery-ablation and paired-comparison evidence narrows the claim
boundary. Constrained discovery has the lowest mean test MAE across the
evaluated model families, but the margin is small and not uniform across
strata. Several numerical-failure rows are retained for transparency and are
not allowed to support positive claims. The multi-season appendix further
weakens any global claim: across completed seasons, pediatric
observation-aware evidence is mixed and season-dependent under the reduced
robustness budget.
