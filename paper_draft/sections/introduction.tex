\section{Introduction}

Forecasting under partial observation is not only a problem of minimizing a
single error column. In influenza hospitalization forecasting, the observed
target is a reported hospitalization-rate time series, while many candidate
models reason in terms of latent quantities such as infectious prevalence,
hospitalized compartments, or delayed observation processes. A model may appear
weak because the latent dynamics are wrong, because the observation map is
wrong, because a simple non-mechanistic baseline is sufficient, or because the
evidence being used does not match the claim being made.

This creates a recommendation problem rather than a pure leaderboard problem.
The central question is:
\begin{quote}
How can agreement and disagreement across model families, proposers, policies,
and evidence modes be used to decide which forecasting recommendations are
credible under partial observation, while preventing leakage and overclaiming?
\end{quote}

We answer this question with a verifier-gated model-selection framework
(Figure~\ref{fig:framework}). The framework treats every model suggestion as a
structured candidate record. Candidates may come from deterministic heuristics,
random orderings, failure-guided refinement, or constrained LLM-backed
proposers, but all candidates enter the same verifier. The verifier enforces
schema validity, explicit allowlists, no-code-generation, no-leakage, budget,
observation-label, and claim-safety constraints. Only verified candidates are
evaluated by deterministic code. Selection policies operate on validation or
rolling-validation evidence, while held-out test metrics are attached only
after selection as post-hoc descriptive evidence.

The contribution is methodological, not a claim of operational flu forecasting
superiority. We make six contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a verifier-gated cross-family model-selection framework for partially
observed forecasting;
\item a constrained agentic proposer protocol with task schemas, explicit
allowlists, JSON candidate records, and verifier feedback;
\item a no-leakage evidence protocol with Pareto-epsilon, weighted-score, and
hard-veto policies plus claim-boundary auditing;
\item synthetic structured recovery experiments that test observation-label
and delay-label recovery under known ground truth;
\item candidate-budget replay and bounded real-data execution experiments that
evaluate proposer ordering without unrestricted refitting; and
\item a FluSurv-NET case study and four-provider agentic proposer benchmark over
OpenAI/GPT, Claude, Gemini, and DeepSeek under the same verifier protocol.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Influenza forecasting and mechanistic epidemic models.}
Operational influenza forecasting has established the value of collaborative
multi-model assessment, real-time evaluation, and ensemble forecasts through
FluSight-style challenges and follow-on systems
\citep{shaman2012forecasting,shaman2013realtime,biggerstaff2016challenge,
mcgowan2019collaborative,reich2019collaborative,reich2019accuracy,
lutz2019pathforward,ray2025flusion}. Mechanistic epidemic modeling provides
interpretable state variables and constrained dynamics
\citep{anderson1991infectious,keeling2008modeling,breto2009mechanistic,
king2016pomp}. Our setting is different from an operational leaderboard:
FluSurv-NET is used as a retrospective case study for credible model
recommendation under partial observation, not as a FluSight or Flusion
replacement.

\paragraph{Partial observation and forecast evaluation.}
Partially observed epidemic time series require an observation model in
addition to latent dynamics; reporting-delay and nowcasting studies make this
separation explicit \citep{king2016pomp,lawless1994reporting,mcgough2020nowcasting}.
Forecast evaluation work motivates out-of-sample, rolling-origin, and
temporally structured validation
\citep{tashman2000outofsample,hyndman2021fpp3,bergmeir2012cv,roberts2017cv}.
Data-leakage studies further motivate separating selection evidence from
held-out test evidence \citep{kaufman2012leakage,kapoor2023leakage}. These
lines motivate our no-leakage rule: held-out test metrics are post-selection
descriptive only.

\paragraph{Symbolic regression and LLM/evaluator scientific discovery.}
Symbolic regression and equation-discovery systems search for interpretable
equations from data
\citep{schmidt2009distilling,brunton2016sindy,udrescu2020aifeynman,
cranmer2023pysr,lacava2021symbolic}. LLM/evaluator systems such as FunSearch,
LLM-SR, and the AI Scientist motivate proposal-and-evaluation loops
\citep{romera2024funsearch,shojaee2024llmsr,lu2024aiscientist}. We borrow the
loop pattern but not unrestricted autonomy: the LLM-backed proposer emits
allowlisted JSON candidate records, while deterministic verifiers and
evaluators control evidence use and claims.

\paragraph{Agentic proposal and budgeted model selection.}
Agentic LLM work studies reasoning-action loops, self-refinement, and
benchmarked agent behavior \citep{yao2023react,madaan2023selfrefine,
liu2023agentbench}. AutoML and budgeted optimization study how to allocate
limited evaluation budgets across candidates
\citep{snoek2012bayesian,feurer2015automl,li2018hyperband,deb2002nsga2}. Our
setting combines these ideas in a narrower protocol: constrained agentic
proposers order cross-family candidates, and Pareto-epsilon, weighted-score,
and hard-veto policies select from non-test evidence.

\paragraph{Adjacent ML-agent and evaluation discipline.}
Closer adjacent work evaluates LLM-backed systems on machine-learning
experimentation and engineering tasks. MLAgentBench and MLE-bench benchmark
agents that design, run, and iterate over ML experiments, while AutoML-GPT and
SELA study LLM-assisted AutoML and search over machine-learning pipelines
\citep{huang2024mlagentbench,chan2025mlebench,zhang2023automlgpt,chi2024sela}.
HELM motivates standardized, multi-metric evaluation of model behavior across
providers and scenarios \citep{liang2023helm}. These references are closer to
our setting than drug-discovery analogies, but our framework is still narrower:
the constrained agentic proposer cannot generate model code, cannot alter the
allowlist, and is evaluated only through verifier-gated proposal quality,
budget efficiency, and claim-bounded replay/execution evidence.
