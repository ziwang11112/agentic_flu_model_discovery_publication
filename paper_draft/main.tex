\title{Verifier-Gated Agentic Model Selection under Partial Observation}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Partially observed forecasting problems make model recommendation difficult:
the same observed series may be consistent with simple statistical baselines,
hand-specified mechanistic models, structured-search candidates, or ablations
that differ mainly in their observation semantics. We study how to make such
recommendations credible without leaking held-out test evidence or overstating
what the data support. We introduce a verifier-gated, evidence-aware,
cross-family model-selection framework. Candidate proposers, including optional
language-model providers, may only emit structured candidate records from an
explicit allowlist. A hard verifier rejects malformed, out-of-allowlist,
leakage-prone, numerically unsafe, or claim-unsafe records. Deterministic
evidence engines then evaluate verified candidates using synthetic execution,
frozen replay, bounded real-data execution, and a FluSurv-NET hospitalization
case study. Selection policies use non-test evidence only, and a claim auditor
separates supported claims from rejected ones. Across synthetic structured
recovery, candidate-budget experiments, a four-provider proposer benchmark, and
FluSurv-NET discovery ablations, the framework supports cautious,
age-dependent and objective-dependent recommendations. The strongest real-data
observation-aware discovery signal appears for pediatric \texttt{0-4 yr};
adult strata often favor simpler baselines; multi-season evidence is mixed.
The paper does not claim state-of-the-art forecasting, a FluSight leaderboard
result, autonomous science, provider clinical superiority, or real-world
mechanism recovery.
\end{abstract}
