\title{Verifier-Gated Evidence-Aware Model Selection\\for Partially Observed Influenza Forecasting}

\begin{abstract}
Partially observed forecasting requires choosing among simple statistical
baselines, hand-specified mechanistic models, structured search candidates,
and ablations without leaking held-out evidence or overstating what the
results support. We present a verifier-gated, evidence-aware, cross-family
model-selection framework that represents proposals as structured candidate
records, checks them with hard allowlist and no-leakage rules, applies
interchangeable policies, and audits claim boundaries. The framework studies
agreement and disagreement across model families, proposers, policies, and
evidence modes. Synthetic structured recovery tests validate observation-label
and delay-label selection logic; candidate-budget replay and bounded execution
evaluate proposal ordering; and a four-provider benchmark compares OpenAI,
Claude, Gemini, and DeepSeek only as constrained structured proposers.
FluSurv-NET hospitalization forecasting is the real-data case study. The
evidence supports age-aware and objective-aware recommendation rather than a
single global winner, with the strongest observation-aware discovery signal in
the pediatric \texttt{0-4 yr} series and mixed multi-season robustness. The
paper does not claim state-of-the-art forecasting, autonomous science, or
real-world mechanism recovery.
\end{abstract}
