\title{Verifier-Gated Cross-Family Model Selection\\Under Partial Observation}
\author{}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Partial observation makes forecasting model recommendation difficult: a series
may favor simple statistical baselines, hand-specified mechanistic models,
structured search candidates, or ablations depending on how the observed
quantity relates to latent dynamics. We present a verifier-gated, evidence-aware
cross-family model-selection framework that represents proposals as structured
candidate records, checks them with allowlist and no-leakage guards, selects
with interchangeable policies, and audits claim boundaries. The language-model
component, when used, is a constrained agentic proposer protocol: providers
receive task schemas, non-test context, verifier feedback, and JSON output
requirements, but cannot generate executable model code or access held-out test
metrics. We validate the framework with synthetic structured recovery tasks for
observation-label and delay-label recovery, candidate-budget execution and
frozen/bounded real-data replay, and a four-provider proposer benchmark over
OpenAI, Claude, Gemini, and DeepSeek under the same verifier protocol.
FluSurv-NET hospitalization forecasting serves as the real-data case study. The
evidence supports age-aware and objective-aware recommendation rather than a
single global winner: the strongest observation-aware discovery signal appears
for pediatric \texttt{0-4 yr}, while adult strata often favor simpler baselines
and multi-season robustness is mixed. The paper does not claim
state-of-the-art forecasting, autonomous science, or real-world mechanism
recovery.
\end{abstract}
