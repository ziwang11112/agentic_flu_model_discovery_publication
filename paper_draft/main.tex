\title{Verifier-Gated Evidence-Aware Model Selection\\for Partially Observed Influenza Forecasting}
\author{}
\date{\today}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Partially observed infectious-disease forecasting requires choosing among
simple statistical baselines, hand-specified mechanistic models, and structured
search candidates without leaking held-out evidence or overstating what the
results support. We present a verifier-gated, evidence-aware selection
framework that converts frozen time-series benchmark summaries into
cross-family candidate records, checks them with hard safety and provenance
rules, applies interchangeable deterministic selection policies, and audits
the resulting claim boundaries. FluSurv-NET hospitalization forecasting is the
real-data case study: the frozen benchmark compares forecasting baselines,
manual epidemic baselines, constrained observation-aware discovery, and
same-grammar ablations. The evidence supports age-aware and objective-aware
selection rather than a single global winner. Additional software evaluations
show that the verifier rejects malformed or claim-unsafe records, an optional
API proposer can produce allowlisted verifier-safe proposals in a small smoke
test, and synthetic structured toy tasks validate observation-label and
delay-label recovery logic without claiming real-world mechanism discovery.
\end{abstract}
